## Aesthetic direction

The visual identity should feel bold, playful, feminine, youthful, and intentionally eclectic. The primary references combine late-1990s/early-2000s editorial design, indie fashion branding, beauty culture, and contemporary collage-style social media graphics.

The site should feel like a small creative fashion/beauty brand rather than a conventional salon website or generic e-commerce template.

### Visual characteristics

- Strong Y2K / early-internet / editorial influence.
- Bold, oversized typography used as a major visual element rather than only for headings.
- Playful editorial layouts inspired by fashion magazines, posters, zines, and social-media collage graphics.
- Bright pink is a dominant brand element, contrasted with deep blue and vivid accent colors.
- High contrast is encouraged. Sections do not need to rely on soft neutral beauty-brand palettes.
- Use irregular or unexpected composition where appropriate: overlapping elements, offset text, stickers, labels, badges, framed content, graphic shapes, and collage-like arrangements.
- Photography should feel integrated into the graphic design rather than simply placed inside generic rectangular cards.
- Rounded elements can be used, but avoid making the entire interface look like a generic modern SaaS/product website.
- Small decorative details may reference print/editorial design: borders, stars, bursts, handwritten or sticker-like accents, oversized words, repeated typography, and intentionally playful graphic elements.
- Some areas may use cleaner/minimal composition to provide breathing room and keep the overall site usable.

### Mood / references

Think:
- Y2K fashion and beauty editorial
- Indie fashion-brand websites
- Fashion magazines and zines
- Scrapbook/collage composition
- Nail-art studio culture
- Pop-art graphics
- Playful maximalism
- Retro print advertising
- Contemporary Instagram/Pinterest fashion branding

The aesthetic can mix polished and intentionally imperfect elements. It should feel designed and expressive rather than corporate or overly clean.

### UX balance

The expressive aesthetic must not compromise usability.

Navigation, booking, product browsing, cart, checkout, forms, prices, appointment times, and important calls to action should remain immediately understandable.

Use the more experimental visual language primarily for:
- Hero sections
- Section transitions
- Promotional content
- Photography
- Decorative elements
- Marketing copy
- Product presentation

Keep transactional interfaces such as checkout, booking forms, waiver forms, and payment states comparatively clear and structured.

### Avoid

Do not default to:
- Generic minimalist salon/spa design
- Beige or muted luxury-beauty aesthetics
- Generic Shopify-style storefront layouts
- Generic SaaS-style cards and dashboards
- Excessive gradients or glassmorphism
- Corporate-looking typography/layouts
- Making every section symmetrical and perfectly uniform
- Overusing rounded white cards on a neutral background
- Making the design overly delicate, elegant, or restrained

The brand should have personality and visual energy while still feeling intentional and professionally designed.
